[warmly] Up to four billion people rely on herbal medicine. Most medicinal plants in trade are still taken from the wild. And almost no one is counting what is left.

In the Philippines, about one hundred seventy-five hectares of tree cover disappear every day. But no one measures harvest. So where should a ranger, or a harvester, even start?

Take tongkat ali. Its roots are dug up and sold. It is officially endangered. Yet open databases hold just five reliable records of it.

[confident] So we built Harvester Risk Atlas: a free map that ranks which wild medicinal plants of the Philippines need help first, where they grow, and how sure we are.

It asks three questions. How rare is it? Does harvesting kill it? Is its home disappearing? Rarity is measured only where botanists actually searched. Then the whole ranking runs two thousand times, with different weights and different data each time. So every plant gets a range, not a single number.
